An LED can communicate several different quantities. Specify which before drawing a light beside every jack.

**Magnitude** indication should observe a buffered signal, with an appropriate rectifier/envelope response when both CV and audio are expected. Equal positive and negative magnitudes can intentionally produce equal brightness; that is not a polarity indicator.

**CLIP** indication needs a threshold related to actual circuit headroom. It is not just a brighter magnitude LED. If a VCA follows a summing stage, monitor upstream clipping as well as the final output where required.

**Stage** indication combines two signals: the generated envelope level and the active rise/fall state. The RISE light may brighten during rise and the FALL light dim during fall; both can be off during sustain even though ENV remains high. The pots set durations, not LED brightness directly.

### Electrical and optical design are inseparable

A small 0402 package may clear a dense PCB while a large light window remains on the faceplate. That still needs a controlled optical path: direct near-panel emitter, diffuser or lightpipe, with no unwanted bleed. Package height can differ between white and red LEDs with similar footprints.

Current must be bounded during normal, startup, tolerance and fault conditions. Use maximum supply, minimum relevant forward voltage and resistor tolerance for a first current screen, then the resistor/LED derating at installed hot ambient. An absolute maximum is not an operating target.

### Layout lesson

Put the emitter/driver where it fits and route the indicator artwork around the real aperture and component keepout. Do not tuck an LED into a corner because it looks empty in a top view while the pot body occupies that volume below.
