The noise block began as one white output and grew to white, pink, blue and brown. These are useful circuit responsibilities to preserve independently of the panel tile.

Electric Druid NOISE2 offers programmed white/pink generation. A blank microcontroller with the same package is not equivalent; programmed identity and delivery must be part of the factory bill of materials. This route is digital pseudorandom generation. A reverse-biased analog junction is a different source with its own repeatability, level and noise-characterization work.

### Shaping within a defined band

Ideal white noise has flat power spectral density. Pink has less high-frequency power; blue has more; brown has a stronger low-frequency weighting. Practical colored-noise outputs must define a useful frequency band and level rather than claiming an ideal spectrum down to DC and up to infinity.

One proposal was leaky/band-limited integration of white for brown and bounded differentiation of pink for blue. The leakage and bandwidth limits prevent uncontrolled DC drift or excessive high-frequency gain. These are proposed topologies, not measured filters or proof of exact coloration.

### Independent outputs

Buffer each output so patch loading does not change the others or the shaping network. Define common level/headroom targets and test spectra, clipping, startup and correlation as appropriate to the musical use.

The corpus retains the NOISE2 source and a separate record for the analog-source direction. It does not invent transistor avalanche ratings or call a noise-colored panel label a finished circuit.
