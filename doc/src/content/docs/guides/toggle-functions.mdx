A switch must be selected from its electrical truth table and its mechanical assembly, not from a generic rectangle in a preview.

| Function | Maintained positions | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LFO / VCO | Two | ON–ON SPDT can encode the two ranges |
| SOFT / OFF / HARD | Three | ON–OFF–ON needs a defined centre-open response |
| ASR / AR / LOOP | Three | Centre-open can mean AR if the circuit defines it |
| Linear / curved envelope | Two | Two states; no invented third browser position |
| Rise / fall stage gate | Two | Select a stage signal; this is not envelope mode |

C908280 refers to the two-position Dailywell candidate. The three-position T1 counterpart is a different complete ordering string. A DPDT option adds another pole and may require more body and terminal space; it is not a stock-based substitute for the SPDT footprint.

### Labels are part of the control contract

A waveform can explain slow versus fast range, a straight versus curved envelope, or the active rise/fall segment. SOFT versus HARD sync is less self-evident; a hybrid of symbols and short words is more reliable than inventing a private icon language. Put full meanings in the documentation even when the panel is compact.

### Assembly and sourcing

PCB pins and solder lugs are different products. Confirm the part's pin view, body, bushing, nut/washer, lever length, throw and soldering/cleaning limits. The corpus keeps the source's unresolved suffix evidence visible rather than equating a shop label with all variants.
