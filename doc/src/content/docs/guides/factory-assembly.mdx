JLCPCB preference and no-home-soldering are related but different constraints. The first chooses a sourcing/assembly route; the second defines the state in which the instrument's electrical assemblies must arrive.

Standard potentiometers, toggles, faders, jacks, programmed devices and externally sourced parts can still satisfy the requirement when the factory accepts their exact procurement and assembly operations. A catalogue entry is not an accepted order, and distributor stock is not reserved PCBA stock.

### Specify the complete delivered assembly

Include soldered through-hole parts, both sides of board connectors, terminated cables, programmed components, any alignment fixture, electrical inspection and the agreed calibration. No adapter or board-to-board joint should quietly be deferred to the user's soldering iron.

Mechanical final assembly—nuts, screws, board supports and plugging completed connectors—can remain separate if that is the agreed delivery arrangement. Full enclosure integration needs its own accepted scope; it cannot be inferred from ordinary PCB assembly service.

### Processes can constrain the part choice

Check the exact component's solder profile, wash restrictions, masking and heat exposure. A plastic actuator that clears the panel may still be incompatible with the assumed assembly process. Ask about the actual orientation and fixture needed for tall or heavy through-hole parts.

### Prototype without committing the whole instrument

A small factory-assembled mechanical coupon can exercise the real jack/nut/panel, pot shaft, rotary selector, toggle, button/plunger and LED optical path. A separate electrical coupon can test a complete oscillator or sample/hold/slew channel. Preserve failures and setup conditions, not only successful photographs.

Source route: [JLCPCB assembly FAQ](https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/pcb-assembly-faqs). Service scope and availability require rechecking at the quotation/order stage; this corpus submits no quote or order.
